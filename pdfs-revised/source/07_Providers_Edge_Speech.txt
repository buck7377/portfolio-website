@title Providers Edge Speech: Short Pitch

A short keynote pitch written in the voice of a healthcare media founder, built around client stories and a four-part framework.

About a year ago, I had a client come to me who had created this very innovative online platform that allows medical professionals to name their own prices for side gigs like telemedicine and speaking engagements instead of going through an agency that takes a huge cut. Brilliant idea, right? I'm sure we all could've benefited from something like that at some point.

But here was the problem: he struggled to get his platform noticed. It just wasn't getting the recognition it deserved. And as a result, he struggled to get users.

Unfortunately, stories like these are all too common. I've seen it again and again. These amazing healthcare startups with incredible ideas just get stuck. It makes me so frustrated!

I'm sure many of you can relate to this exact situation. Maybe you've had some success with your business so far. You've landed a few contracts here and there with organizations looking to meet their healthcare needs. Things seem to be going alright.

But despite your early wins, you just can't seem to find the ideal approach to bring in clients consistently. And even when potential customers do come your way, many don't end up converting at the rate you had hoped.

It's frustrating, right? You know your solutions are top-notch and can make a real difference. But it feels like you've hit a plateau. Growth has stagnated. You're not sure what to do next to get to the next level.

I've been there too. But here's the good news: strategic partnerships can change everything. With the right alliances, you can attract a steady stream of qualified leads and turn those leads into long-term customers.

Just like my client who created the side gig platform for medical professionals. I was excited to get on board and help because I saw his product was meeting a real need I had experienced before. He just wasn't getting the recognition he deserved.

So I stepped in to help give him and his business more visibility. I worked to get him coverage in the media and set up strategic partnerships. And you know what happened? After just a little bit of collaboration, he had a million-dollar fundraising round coming his way.

The same thing can happen for you and your business.

But hey, trying to form partnerships can be daunting sometimes, right?

Does this sound like you? You show up to a networking event but struggle to get people interested or make meaningful connections. You feel frustrated because you don't know what to say, and you keep finding yourself repeating the same elevator pitch over and over. And even when you do meet promising contacts, they often disappear.

I get it. Putting yourself out there can be nerve-wracking. But don't worry, I have a solution.

I've created a simple strategy I call the Four Cs to attract and keep high-value strategic partners who will become champions of your mission:

<b>The first C stands for Captivate Intensely.</b> The key is brevity. Be concise and focused when introducing yourself. Hook their interest quickly. Lead with your mission and values. Give them a compelling reason to want to learn more.

<b>The second C stands for Create Strategic Partnerships.</b> Identify organizations that serve similar clients but in different ways. Research where those potential partners gather. Reach out and position yourself as a trusted resource.

<b>The third C stands for Collaborate for Mutual Benefit.</b> Co-create initiatives that help both parties. Make it easy for partners to understand your offerings through simple materials. Continue nurturing relationships and stay top of mind.

<b>And finally, the fourth C stands for Calibrate Inquisitively.</b> Keep evaluating and enhancing your people skills. Study body language, listening tactics, and rapport building. Be open to feedback on how you communicate and connect.

Now, let me tell you about the incredible impact I've seen these strategies have.

One of my clients has a comprehensive software platform that remotely engages patients, tracks their biometrics, and creates personalized care plans. Their company already has over 600,000 patients using it and steady revenue.

But when the founder first came to me, he struggled with high-level communications. Through evaluating and enhancing his people skills, we transformed how he connected with potential partners. After just a bit of honing his communication tactics, he was able to land a partnership for a major billing system integration that cut payment processing time in half.

The same kind of breakthrough can happen for you and your business. When you captivate the right people, create strategic partnerships, collaborate for mutual benefit, and keep calibrating your communication skills, you too can start to accelerate your success and change more lives through strategic "win-win" partnerships.
